3. Plasma current, phase lag, and collisional heating
The bounded first model is linear collisional electron response at frozen density. It resolves electron inertia and therefore distinguishes reversible drift storage from irreversible heat. It excludes ionization, flow, pressure-driven currents, relativistic motion and kinetic resonances.

Derive the current equation from electron momentum
Take e>0 as the elementary charge magnitude and a nonnegative effective collision frequency nu. With J_p=-f_p n_e e v_e and fixed f_p n_e, multiplying the electron momentum equation gives the causal current response. At f_p n_e=0, set J_p=K_p=q_nu=0 without dividing by zero. [3]
m_e(\partial_t+\nu)\mathbf v_e=-e\mathbf E,\qquad \alpha=\frac{f_p n_e e^2}{m_e}=f_p\epsilon_0\omega_p^2
\partial_t\mathbf J_p+\nu\mathbf J_p=\alpha\mathbf E,\qquad \omega_p^2=\frac{n_e e^2}{\epsilon_0m_e}

The measurable amplitude and phase response
For harmonic peak phasors with exp(-i omega t), omega>0 and coefficients held fixed for the response, conductivity is complex. Its positive phasor angle means current lags electric field in time under this convention; at nu=0 the lag is pi/2. The instantaneous current equation remains authoritative for finite pulses or varying temperature; the conductivity is a frozen-coefficient harmonic diagnostic.
\sigma_p(\omega)=\frac{\alpha}{\nu-i\omega},\qquad |\widehat{\mathbf J}_p|=\frac{\alpha|\widehat{\mathbf E}|}{\sqrt{\nu^2+\omega^2}}
\Delta\theta_{J,E}=\mathrm{atan2}(\omega,\nu),\qquad \epsilon_r(\omega)=1-\frac{f_p\omega_p^2}{\omega(\omega+i\nu)}

Storage and irreversible heat
Dot the current equation with J_p/alpha. This gives the following identity exactly for fixed alpha, including temperature-dependent nu>=0. Instantaneous J_p dot E can be negative when current returns stored energy to the wave; it must not all be called heat. [4]
K_p=\frac{|\mathbf J_p|^2}{2\alpha},\qquad q_\nu=\frac{\nu|\mathbf J_p|^2}{\alpha}\geq0
\mathbf J_p\cdot\mathbf E=\partial_tK_p+q_\nu
\langle q_\nu\rangle=\frac{\nu|\widehat{\mathbf J}_p|^2}{2\alpha}=\frac12\mathrm{Re}(\sigma_p)|\widehat{\mathbf E}|^2

A thermal closure
For a common, locally equilibrated ideal plasma temperature T_p, let C_p=(3/2) f_p(n_e+n_i) k_B, with n_i=n_e/Z. This single-temperature closure requires equilibration on the thermal time scale; otherwise use separate species energies and heat partition. k_p,eff is cell-effective thermal conductivity. Q_pc is heat transferred from plasma to cover per cell volume [W/m^3], entered with opposite signs. Set g_pc=0 for the insulated-contact benchmark, or calibrate g_pc=h_pc A_interface/V_cell from interface conductance h_pc [W/(m^2 K)]. Its support requires both media. Constant nu is a controlled first benchmark; a positive nu(T_p) must be calibrated.
C_p\partial_tT_p=\nabla\cdot(k_{p,\rm eff}\nabla T_p)+q_\nu-Q_{pc}
Q_{pc}=g_{pc}(T_p-T_c),\qquad g_{pc}\geq0

Enthalpy carries the energy of melting

Geometric cover is a separate observable
