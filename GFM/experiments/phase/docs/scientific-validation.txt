GFM RESEARCH SIMULATOR: SCIENTIFIC ACCEPTANCE AND LIMITS
Prepared 2026-10-11; research-revision numerical assessment

1. DECISION AND MODEL SCOPE

The intended use is a local research prototype for comparing a stated
aperture/cover hypothesis with reference Kerr geometry and numerical null
test-light paths. It supports equation inspection and reproducible numerical
experiments. It has no accepted empirical domain for an astronomical source.
Mission, flight, material-safety, and physical-traversability decisions are
outside the demonstrated scope. No NASA certification is claimed.

The model has distinct layers:

  Reference geometry: stationary, neutral Kerr, prescribed mass and spin.
  Null paths: massless test particles on that background; no backreaction.
  Hypothesis: spin-dependent toy aperture and assumed cover threshold.
  Representation: complex trace-character transform indexed by GF9 labels.
  Display: coordinate embedding and an illustration clock.

The cover does not enter the Kerr metric or the null-path equations. Both
branches therefore have the same reference spacetime and ray bundle at the
same mass, spin, and ray settings. This is a modeling choice. It does not show
that a real massive cover could be added without changing a gravitational field.
No formation, Einstein evolution, radiative transfer, Maxwell, GRMHD, or
material constitutive equations are supplied by this model.

Verification asks whether the code solves the declared model correctly.
Analytic benchmarks compare numerical outputs with independently calculated
solutions or limits. Empirical validation would compare the model with relevant
observations and their measurement process. These are separate evidence types.
Screenshots, successful controls, and GPU rendering establish software/display
behavior; they do not establish physical accuracy.

3. COORDINATES, EQUATIONS AND NUMERICAL DOMAIN

Use signature (-,+,+,+), Boyer-Lindquist (t,r,theta,phi), G=c=1,
Sigma=r^2+a^2*cos(theta)^2, Delta=r^2-2Mr+a^2, a/M=spin.
The neutral spacetime charge is Q_e=0. Carter's constant Q_C is a different
quantity and must never be identified with electric charge.

For positive photon energy E normalized to one, write L=Lz/E and eta=Q_C/E^2:

  R(r)=[r^2+a^2-aL]^2-Delta*[eta+(L-a)^2]
  Theta(theta)=eta+a^2*cos(theta)^2-L^2*cot(theta)^2.

Mino parameter tau satisfies dx/dtau=Sigma*p^mu/E. The implementation's
chain-rule variables are u=1/r and mu=cos(theta); then u'^2=u^4*R(1/u)
and mu'^2=(1-mu^2)*Theta(arccos(mu)). Primes mean d/dtau.
Second-order equations derived from these first integrals naturally pass
through regular turning points, but numerical constraint error must still be
measured. E, L and eta are fixed inputs; simply reprinting them is not evidence
of conservation. Momentum reconstructed from the integrated velocities provides
an additional, non-tautological check.
Equation source: Gralla and Lupsasca (2020), equations 1-9, with the project's
explicit variable changes. https://arxiv.org/html/1910.12881v2

The implemented adaptive method is Dormand-Prince 5(4) on the separated
equations. Exported settings include tolerance, maxStep, maxMino, maxSteps,
maxPoints and horizonGuard. The internal local tolerance is
min(0.02*tolerance,2e-9). Default maxStep=0.025/M, maxMino=10/M,
maxSteps=12000, maxPoints=600 and horizonGuard=0.02M. The numerical step floor
is 1e-13/M; exhaustion has its own unresolved outcome. Direct-call bounds
include M=0.01..100, spin=0..1, tolerance=1e-11..1e-4, maxSteps=10..50000
and maxPoints=4..5000. These input bounds are not an accuracy guarantee.
Paths end before the Boyer-Lindquist horizon/polar coordinate singularities;
an exterior termination guard is not a continuation through the horizon.
Step exhaustion, a coordinate guard, and an unresolved near-critical path need
their own outcomes and must not be silently relabeled as physical capture.

An exact unstable radial separatrix previously acquired a false numerical
capture despite tiny first-integral residuals. That defect was corrected and
regression checked. The corrected solver examines stationary points of V=u^4R
over the planned radial range. If abs(V)/max(1,sum(abs(V terms))) is at most
max(64*machine_epsilon,100*internalTolerance), it reports critical-limit and
censors an uncertainty neighborhood sqrt(2*absoluteBand/abs(Vsecond)). This is
a conservative numerical classification band, not a physical shell thickness.
Its diagnostics record the stationary roots, gap, band and guard. Exact and
nearby cases are tested independently; no capture/escape claim is accepted
inside this band. Coordinate-limited and critical-limit paths remain unresolved.

The UI's current worker configuration permits observerRadius 12..100 M,
inclination 15..165 degrees, 8..32 rays, and tolerance 1e-10..1e-4. These are
interface bounds observed in backend/physics-service.cjs, not a validated
accuracy domain. The solver may offer a larger direct-call test domain.
Finite-radius initial conditions using asymptotic alpha,beta impact parameters
are not a local orthonormal observer tetrad or a calibrated camera projection.

Display mapping, with y as the polar axis:
  x=sqrt(r^2+a^2)*sin(theta)*cos(phi)
  y=r*cos(theta)
  z=sqrt(r^2+a^2)*sin(theta)*sin(phi).
The mapping specifies coordinate shape, not proper spatial distance. A display
animation parameter must not be called photon travel time unless it is tied to
an explicitly exported time coordinate and a stated mass scale.

5. UNCERTAINTY AND IDENTIFIABILITY

Numerical uncertainty includes floating-point cancellation, accumulated solver
error, event/guard localization, finite-radius endpoints, and ray sampling.
The discrete bundle does not provide a converged image or intensity field.
Do not turn a local tolerance or single conservation residual into a percentage
accuracy claim for the whole simulation.
The independent checker reconstructs pointwise first integrals and prescribed
coordinate derivatives from exported velocities. It does not independently
integrate every Kerr coordinate or provide a full pathwise phase-error bound.
Azimuth has an independent complete-scattering benchmark in the tested
Schwarzschild cases; a quantitative inclined-Kerr image would require further
coordinate/phase convergence and an explicit observation/emission model.

Model uncertainty includes omitted matter and emission physics, the prescribed
stationary background, and the unverified aperture/cover law. Its parameters
are controls, not measured estimates with observational confidence intervals.
Near the aperture critical spin, dr_A/dspin grows without bound on the open
side; a cover threshold has a discontinuous passage bit. Small parameter
perturbations can therefore alter the hypothesis outcome. Log threshold-side
tests and do not smooth them into evidence for a physical transition.

Identifiability depends on the observation map. Structural identifiability
asks whether distinct parameters can produce distinguishable outputs even
with ideal data. For the declared equal-exterior branches, equal likelihoods
give posterior=prior by Bayes' rule. Different hidden labels cannot be recovered
by applying an invertible transform to identical exterior inputs. A protocol
that observes assumed passage has different information and different premises.
Primary methodology reference: Chis, Banga and Balsa-Canto (2011):
https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0027755

EHT's M87 analysis fitted visibility data and calibrated models involving an
emission region. That is an example of the observational work this project
has not performed. Similar visual appearance is not an observational fit,
and those results do not validate this project's aperture or cover hypothesis.
Primary example: EHT Collaboration (2019), M87 Results VI:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11243
